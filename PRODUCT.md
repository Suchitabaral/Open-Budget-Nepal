# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Citizens, researchers, journalists, civil-society organizations, and government officials exploring Nepal's public budgets, procurement, fiscal transfers, contractors, and fiscal risks. Their primary task is to move from a national overview to verifiable federal, provincial, or local detail without losing context.

## Product Purpose

Open Budget Nepal makes public-finance records understandable, searchable, and reusable. Success means people can identify where money was budgeted, what was actually spent, how results differ across government levels, and which underlying records support each conclusion.

## Brand Personality

Trustworthy, restrained, and accountable. The interface speaks plainly, prioritizes evidence, and feels like a dependable public instrument rather than a campaign or consumer product.

## Anti-references

Avoid flashy gradients, glassmorphism, ornamental government imagery, excessive color, generic SaaS card grids, and dashboards that show controls or precision unsupported by the underlying data. Do not reproduce the World Bank dashboard's visual styling; use its clear fiscal hierarchy only as an information-architecture reference.

## Design Principles

- Make the source and scope of every number apparent.
- Organize analysis around Nepal's federal, provincial, and local government structure.
- Reveal detailed controls progressively instead of presenting every option at once.
- Prefer familiar, accessible controls and plain language.
- Never imply that mock, estimated, or unavailable data is authoritative.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.1 AA contrast and keyboard access, visible focus states, reduced-motion support, color-independent chart identification, responsive layouts, and readable data tables for people using assistive technology.
